# Issuer CSD and investor CSD — what the terms mean and why one CSD can be both

**Question.** In plain terms, what is the difference between an issuer CSD and an investor CSD, and can a CSD such as Monte Titoli be both at the same time?

**Direct answer.** The two terms describe a CSD's role for one particular securities issue, not a permanent label. The issuer CSD is the CSD where the issue is recorded and maintained; an investor CSD is a CSD that holds that same issue through another CSD's system in order to let its own participants settle it. Because the role is fixed per issue and per relationship, one CSD is routinely the issuer CSD for the securities issued with it and an investor CSD for foreign securities it makes available through links. Yes, Monte Titoli can be both at the same time, for different ISINs and even for the same transaction chain in T2S.

## Documented requirements

1. **Legal definitions (EU).** The settlement RTS defines the issuer CSD as "a CSD which provides the core service referred to in point 1 or 2 of Section A of the Annex to Regulation (EU) No 909/2014 in relation to a securities issue" (the notary or central-maintenance service), and the investor CSD as "a CSD that either is a participant in the securities settlement system operated by another CSD or that uses a third party or an intermediary that is a participant in the securities settlement system operated by another CSD in relation to a securities issue". [[rts392-issuer-investor-csd]] Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/392, Article 1(e)–(f), OJ L 65 of 10 March 2017; English text captured from the original act on 14 September 2026; reviewed 14 September 2026 (reference mode: partial browser excerpt; EU official languages). The phrase "in relation to a securities issue" in both definitions is what makes the role relational.

2. **How T2S assigns the roles.** For the realignment process, a CSD is defined as "the issuer CSD, when it is the CSD in which the security has been issued and distributed on behalf of the Issuer; the investor CSD, when it is the CSD of at least one party of the Settlement Instruction; or both, when it is the CSD in which the security has been issued and the CSD of at least one party of the Settlement Instruction". [[t2s-realignment]] T2S UDFS R2026.JUN, §1.6.1.10.3 "Role and links between CSDs", PDF 375; reviewed 13 September 2026. The UDFS therefore states expressly that a single CSD can be "both" in one settlement.

3. **How an investor CSD holds the securities.** Each investor CSD chooses between "opening an omnibus account in the books of the issuer CSD to reflect the holdings of its participants for the securities" or "opening an omnibus account in the books of any other CSD being already an investor CSD for the same financial instrument"; the CSD where that omnibus account is opened is the investor CSD's "technical issuer" for that ISIN. Only one link per technical issuer, investor CSD and ISIN may exist at a time; one link is the "default" and others may be "alternative". [[t2s-realignment]] UDFS §1.6.1.10.3, PDF 375–376; reviewed 13 September 2026.

4. **What happens when participants of two CSDs settle.** Cross-CSD settlement "is achieved in T2S with the simultaneous booking of cash and securities for Settlement Instructions between participants of different CSDs"; once the business instructions are matched, T2S "creates automatically all the requested Settlement Instructions between the involved CSDs" (realignment instructions) based on the links in reference data, and "ensures that the T2S generated realignment Settlement Instructions and their business Settlement Instructions settle on an all-or-none basis". [[t2s-realignment]] UDFS §1.6.1.10.1–1.6.1.10.2, PDF 373–375; reviewed 13 September 2026. Posting, not matching, is what moves positions: the posting process "checks if the settlement … can be achieved considering their eligibility to settlement and the available resources" and its booking results "in the irrevocability of the settlement". [[t2s-posting]] UDFS §1.6.1.8.1–1.6.1.8.2, PDF 303–304; reviewed 13 September 2026.

5. **Monte Titoli's own rule.** For cross-CSD settlement "T2S shall automatically carry out the movements between the securities accounts of the participants involved, of the Investor CSDs and of the Issuer CSD", and Monte Titoli "does not envisage the possibility of carrying out a cross CSD settlement on securities if the Issuer CSD is outside of T2S, unless both investor CSDs have in place a link with another CSD in T2S so that the realignment with the Issuer CSD outside T2S is not necessary". [[milan-cross-csd-disclosure]] Monte Titoli Service Regulations of 26 January 2026, Article 77(1)–(2), PDF 54 (printed 53); reviewed 14 September 2026; English translation, the Italian text prevails.

6. **A second local illustration (Porto).** Euronext Securities Porto describes foreign securities "issued in a foreign CSD" that are held "through indirect links with foreign CSDs, through the Interlinking Financial Intermediary (participant) model", recorded in a "Registration and Control Account" that mirrors the position at the foreign depository, and separately direct T2S links with named CSDs. [[porto-cross-csd-links]] Interbolsa Operational Manual V43 (internal date 26 January 2026), Chapter 15, PDF 183–184 (printed 182–183); reviewed 14 September 2026; reference mode — English version, Portuguese text governs, manual effective date unresolved.

## Plain-language explanation

- Think of the issuer CSD as the register of record for an issue: the total issued amount lives there. An investor CSD is a CSD that wants its participants to hold and settle that issue without becoming direct participants of the issuer CSD. It opens an omnibus (pooled) account with the issuer CSD, or with another CSD that already holds the issue, and mirrors that pooled balance in its own books for its participants.
- **Reasoned inference from items 3–4:** when a participant of the investor CSD buys from a participant of the issuer CSD, the issuer CSD's books move securities from the seller's account into the investor CSD's omnibus account, while the investor CSD's books credit the buyer. Those are two layers of the same position, not a second issuance; T2S generates the inter-CSD "realignment" legs itself and settles them together with the trade.
- **Reasoned inference from items 1–2 and 5:** Monte Titoli is the issuer CSD for securities issued with it (for example Italian government bonds recorded there) and an investor CSD when its participants hold securities issued at another CSD through Monte Titoli's links. Article 77 shows the group behaving as an investor CSD (it speaks of "the Investor CSDs" and of "the Issuer CSD outside of T2S"), and the T2S definition allows one CSD to be both in the same chain.
