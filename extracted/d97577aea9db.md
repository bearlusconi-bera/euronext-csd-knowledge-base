1.1 Background and objectives


The purpose of the procedure is to ensure that Euronext Securities Oslo (or “ES-OSL”)
takes the appropriate measures in a default situation of a participant. The procedure
covers the types of actions ES-OSL may take in case of insolvency of a participant and
how ES-OSL shall test and periodically review the procedure.

According to CSDR (Central Securities Depository Regulation) art. 41.2 and the ESMA
(European Securities and Markets Authority) Guidelines on Participant Default Rules and
Procedures under CSDR, the procedure regarding a situation where insolvency
proceedings are opened against a participant shall be (I) approved by the CSD’s
management body and (II) made available to the public. Thus, this insolvency procedure
(the “Procedure”) was formally approved by the Euronext Securities (ES-OSL) Board of
Directors on 22 June 2020 and subsequently in 2025.

Rules on participants default are included in the VPO NOK rules. This procedure describes
in further detail the actions required if a participant of VPO NOK becomes subject to
insolvency proceedings as defined in Section 23 of the VPO NOK rules. A participant is a
party approved by ES-OSL as a participant in VPO NOK, including CCPs and other CSDs.
The procedure also describes the actions related to the same party’s activity as a
settlement bank and a liquidity bank, as defined in section 4 of the VPO NOK Rules.

In order to be registered as a participant in VPO NOK, it is a prerequisite to also be an
account operator. This authorisation may be limited to their own accounts only; however,
most settlement participants also act as account operators for their clients’ accounts. In
the event of insolvency, the authorisation as an account operator will be revoked.
However, the revocation of discontinuation of account operator authorisation is not
addressed by this procedure but is outlined in the “Euronext Securities Oslo Rules for
Registration Activity and Related Ancillary Services”.

1.2 About this procedure


This procedure describes the following steps:

1.  Notification and verification of the default of a participant.

   a) SIP and internal escalation.

   b) Notification and verification.

    c)  Confirmation and documentation.

2. Communication and notification following the default of a participant.
3.  Actions to be taken in case of a default of a participant.
4.  Testing of the procedure.
5.  Training of personnel.

1.3 Timing


1. Execution of the procedure.

The procedure is to be executed ad-hoc when insolvency proceedings are opened against

This procedure is to be tested on an annual basis. The results of the test must be sent to
the authorities and other relevant parties described in this procedure.

ES-OSL should activate its participants default rules and procedures once ES-OSL, after
having taken all reasonable steps to verify its occurrence, has identified (or confirmed) a
participant as default.

1.1 SIP (Serious Incident process) and
internal escalation


1.  Serious Incident Process.

   •   After ES-OSL (and anyone within the company) receives information that a
       participant has been placed under insolvency proceedings or that this is imminent,
     ES-OSL should immediately summon a Serious Incident Procedure (SIP).

   •  The notification and verification steps (next section of this document) of a
       participant’s default should not be postponed pending the establishment of the
      SIP.

   •  The SIP is responsible for the activities described in the sections below and to
      coordinate the activities with other entities or persons mentioned in the procedure.

2.  Escalation to internal stakeholders.

   •   If anyone in ES-OSL receives information that a participant has been placed under
      insolvency proceedings or that this is imminent, this should immediately be
      escalated to the Head of Product and Head of Legal.

   •   If the Head of Product and Head of Legal are not available, the CEO or others in
     Management must be informed, and immediate action must be taken to clarify the
       situation to determine whether an insolvency situation exists.

   •  The time and date when the insolvency proceedings or the default was finally

ES-OSL must take the below actions in case of a default participants. These actions are
measures ES-OSL takes to contain losses and liquidity pressures, at and after the point
of participant default.

ES-OSL should specify whether those actions are automatic or decided on a case-by-case
basis.

In short, ES-OSL must change the normal settlement practice, blocking the entry of
additional settlement instructions in the VPO NOK by the defaulting participant,
suspending its non-final settlement instructions from settlement and/or restricting certain
functionalities that can be applied to the settlement instructions of that participant.

Before those actions are taken, ES-OSL must perform the below:

3.1 Identification of roles, description of
selection criteria


1.  Identify roles the default participant has towards ES-OSL.

   •  Type(s) of authorisations the default participant has from ES-OSL.

   •  And other agreements between the default participant and ES-OSL

2. Describe selection criteria and consequences of actions

ES-OSL must specify the consequences of the actions ES-OSL may take in respect of
defaulting and non-defaulting participants’ settlement instructions and accounts.

ES-OSL must specify the criteria for selection of actions listed in the procedure: ES-OSL
must specify the criteria to be taken into account for the choice of each of the actions
listed in its default rules and procedures.

3.2 Exclude the default participant from VPO
NOK


ES-OSL has the right to withdraw the approval as a participant and the approval as a
liquidity bank, including setting conditions for continued authorisation if the participant is
placed under insolvency proceedings.

1.  Decision to exclude the default participant.

After verifying a default, the CEO should decide whether to exclude the participant from
further participation in VPO NOK—either fully or partially, temporarily or permanently. If
the CEO is unavailable, this decision should be made jointly by the Head of Product and
the Head of Legal.

   •  Decision-making mandate to exclude the participant:

3.3 Settle, block, defer, and remove
instructions


1.  Block additional settlement instructions

Block the entry of additional settlement instructions in the VPO NOK by the defaulting
participant.

2. Settlement of settlement instructions

Settlement instructions that have already been entered into the VPO NOK at the time
that the participant enters insolvency proceedings, will still be processed in the
settlement cycle. This applies to settlement instructions submitted to VPO NOK until the
point in time at which ES-OSL is officially notified about the insolvency by the NFSA or
another public authority.

Settlement of same settlement day instructions
More specifically, settlement instructions that are scheduled for the same settlement day
the participant enters insolvency proceedings will be included in settlement for same
settlement day. Any other settlement instructions scheduled for future dates (not the
same settlement day) will be deferred.

3.  Sufficient financial instruments and cash (liquidity) available to cover the instructions

   •  Settlement instructions will be settled provided there are sufficient financial
      instruments and cash (liquidity) available to cover the instructions.

   •  Settlement instructions for which there is insufficient financial instruments and/or
       liquidity will be deferred.

3.4 Transfer of Settlement Instructions to a
Participant’s Substitute Liquidity Bank


1. When to transfer the settlement instruments to its substitute liquidity bank:

If the defaulting party is a liquidity bank for other participants, these participant’s
settlement instructions will be transferred to its substitute liquidity bank. This applies
from the first settlement cycle that starts after ES-OSL becomes aware of the default of
the liquidity bank.

2. When to transfer the settlement instruments to its substitute liquidity bank:

If the default is confirmed after a settlement cycle has started, the settlement
instructions that have been entered for that cycle will not be transferred to the substitute
liquidity bank.

3.  Notification of transferred settlement instructions

ES-OSL shall notify settlement participants and liquidity banks in VPO NOK that it has
transferred settlement instructions to a substitute liquidity bank as soon as possible.

ES-OSL must test the effectiveness and practicality of this procedure.

1. Scope of the test.

The test is performed namely on sections 1, 2, and 3, i.e. the notification and verification
of a default participant, the communication to NFSA and relevant parties, and the actions
to be taken in case of a default participant. The competent authority may request to
participate in these tests.

2.  Responsibilities in conducting the test.
ES-OSL is responsible to ensure this test is conducted in compliance with the
requirements.

3. Content of the test.

The test of the participant default procedure should include:

   •  a simulation exercise

   •  and a test of the communication procedures.


4. Timing of the test

   •  Annually (minimum)

   •  ad-hoc after substantive change to the default rules, or

   •  upon request from the authorities.

4.1 Participants involved in the test


When conducting this test, ES-OSL must involve a relevant sample of its participants.
Hence, ES-OSL must engage with the below:

1. Relevant market infrastructures, such as:

   •  Other CSDs.

   •   Entities settling the cash leg of securities transactions settled through ES-OSL

   •   Central Counterparties (CCPs).

2. Any other key entities, such as:

   •   Third-party service providers to which the provision of services has been
      outsourced

   •  Operators of common settlement infrastructure, if any.

4.2 Preparatory work for the test


Prior to each test, ES-OSL should prepare in advance with the below steps:

1. Define the parameters according to which such test should be run, taking into
   account the below:

   •   different types of participants: in terms of volume, activity, etc.

   •   different types of accounts held by the participants: omnibus and segregated.

   •   relevant market infrastructures: refer to 2.5.2 CSDs.

If requested by the competent authority, ES-OSL may submit the parameters it intends
to use to its competent authority prior to each test.

2.  Specify the below aspects:

   •  mechanism, timing, parameters, scope of the testing, purpose of the test (i.e. test
      the effectiveness and practicality of the procedure).

4.3 Perform the test


ES-OSL must perform a test of this procedure (sections 1,2 and 3) which involves the
below:

1. A simulation exercise and
2. A test of the communication procedures.

The simulation test involves using the DEMO system, where the “Procedure for SQL step-
by-step to remove the participant” is used. This test puts weight on having valuable
discussions that could benefit in clearing up topics that is unclear. ES-OSL must involve
the relevant employees and departments to conduct this test.

The test of the communication procedures is scheduled between the different
stakeholders and is rescheduled within the calendar year in the case where if any are
prevented to participate. Inform the selected participants in due time with the relevant
information necessary for them to participate in this test.

4.4 Results of the test - Report


The outcomes of the test are written down in a report.

This report must include results from the simulation test and the test of communication
procedures, as specified below:

1. From the simulation exercise: challenges faced and potential improvements.

    APPENDIX:
  DEFINITIONS

1.1 Key participants


Key participants as defined by RTS 2017/392, Article 67.

1. A CSD shall, on an ongoing basis, identify the key participants in the securities
   settlement system that it operates based on the following factors:

(a) their transaction volumes and values;

(b) material dependencies between its participants and its participants' clients, where the
clients are known to the CSD, that might affect the CSD;

(c) their potential impact on other participants and the securities settlement system of
the CSD as a whole in the event of an operational problem affecting the smooth provision
of services by the CSD.

For the purposes of point (b) in the first subparagraph, the CSD shall also identify the
following:

                       (i) the participants' clients responsible for a significant proportion of
                     transactions processed by the CSD;
                         (ii) the participants' clients whose transactions, based on their volumes
                 and values, are significant relative to the respective participants'
                  risk-management capacity.

(a) for financial instruments referred to in Article 3(1) of Regulation (EU) No 600/2014 of
the European Parliament and of the Council (7) admitted to trading on a trading venue
within the Union, the market value shall be the closing price of the most relevant market
in terms of liquidity referred to in Article 4(6)(b) of that Regulation;

(b) for financial instruments admitted to trading on a trading venue within the Union
other than those referred to in point (a), the market value shall be the closing price
derived from the trading venue within the Union with the highest turnover;

(c) for financial instruments other than those referred to in points (a) and (b) the market
value shall be determined on the basis of a price calculated using a pre-determined
methodology that refers to criteria related to market data, such as market prices
available across trading venues or investment firms.
